Represented artists

Thirty-one people
who make the work

Painters, printmakers and photographers, most of them working within a day's travel of the gallery. We visit every studio at least twice a year, choose the pieces together with the artist, and keep the wall small on purpose. Four studios are shown below in detail. The rest of the roster appears in the current collection.

01

Mireille Okonjo

Painter · Oil on linen · Lisbon

Mireille paints interiors from memory, then sands them back until the first drawing shows through. Canvases arrive stretched on poplar and are usually large. She has shown with us since 2014 and releases eight to ten paintings a year, no more.

  • Afternoon, Rua da Bica 2023 · 140 × 110 cm
  • Two Chairs, One Window 2022 · 90 × 70 cm
  • Verso No. 4 2024 · 120 × 90 cm

02

Anders Holm

Printmaker · Drypoint, lithography · Copenhagen

Anders pulls his own editions on a 1961 Fuchs press, rarely more than twenty-five prints per plate. The plates are copper, the paper is Hahnemühle, and every sheet is signed in pencil in the lower margin. Editions sell out quickly, so we hold one artist proof of each for the archive.

  • Harbour Crane I to IV Ed. 25 · drypoint
  • Night Ferry Ed. 18 · lithograph
  • Salt Field Ed. 12 · drypoint
See available editions
03

Junko Saitō

Photographer · Silver gelatin · Kyoto

Junko photographs the same three riverbanks through the year on 6 × 7 film and prints in her own darkroom. Sizes are modest, usually 30 × 40 cm, and each print is selenium toned and mounted on museum board. She sends us a folder of new work each spring.

  • Kamo River, February Ed. 10 · 30 × 40 cm
  • Low Water Ed. 10 · 40 × 50 cm
  • Bridge, Third Visit Ed. 6 · 30 × 40 cm
Request the print list
04

Rafael Ibarra

Painter · Egg tempera on panel · Oaxaca

Rafael grinds his own pigment and paints on gessoed birch panels no larger than a hand-span. The surfaces are matte and take months. He joined the roster in 2019 after we saw fourteen panels laid out on a kitchen table, and he still prefers to be seen that way, in groups of four or six.

  • Small Weather I to VI 2024 · 18 × 14 cm each
  • Terracotta Study 2023 · 24 × 18 cm
  • Reverse of a Door 2022 · 30 × 22 cm

How representation works here

A short roster, looked after properly

We take on two or three new artists a year and only when we can sell the work honestly. Every studio gets a written agreement, a fixed split, and payment within fourteen days of a sale. Pricing is set with the artist and stays the same whether a piece sells through the viewing room or online.

Framing happens at our bench on Hudson Street using conservation glass and acid-free mounts. Each work leaves with a certificate signed by the artist and a condition photograph for your records.
